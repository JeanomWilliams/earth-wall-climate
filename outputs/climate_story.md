# Abuja's hot-dry season, read from the data

*Every figure below is calculated by `climate_summary.py` from the NASA POWER file.*

## The hours

Across 89 days, from 1 February to 30 April 2026, the air in Abuja was recorded in hourly steps
2,136 times. The day has a clear rhythm. Air is coolest at 05:00, when the average
temperature is 23.2 °C. It climbs through the morning and peaks at 13:00, when the
average reaches 36.7 °C. That is 13.5 °C between the
coolest and hottest hour of an average day, and 8 hours from the coolest hour to the peak.

## The nights

Night does not bring much relief. At 05:00 the average is 23.2 °C, and
on the coolest night of the season the air fell to 18.8 °C (01 Mar).
Those nights are when a building can release the heat it has stored during the day, which is the
window that night ventilation uses.

## The days

The hottest day was 16 Feb, when the air reached 41.0 °C. The widest
daily swing was on 08 Mar, at 19.9 °C, and the narrowest on
31 Mar, at 5.2 °C. The average daily swing across the season is
13.6 °C. A building designed for the average day will meet days that are very
different from it.

## The months

- February averaged 29.5 °C.
- March averaged 30.3 °C.
- April averaged 28.4 °C.

March was the warmest month and April the coolest. The season does not
simply get hotter; the pattern shifts through the spring.

## What this means for building

The climate sets the problem. The outdoor air swings by about 13 °C
in an average day, and nights stay warm. Any wall in Abuja has to deal with heat arriving in the
afternoon and a cooling period at night. This summary describes the outdoor conditions only. How a
particular wall responds, and whether the room stays comfortable, depends on ventilation, windows,
roof and occupancy, which are not part of this climate data.

## Source and limits

NASA POWER hourly 2 m air temperature (T2M), derived from MERRA-2 reanalysis. Reanalysis is
model-based, not a station measurement. Local standard time is used throughout.
